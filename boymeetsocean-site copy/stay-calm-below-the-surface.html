Learning to Stay Calm Below the Surface

When I first started spending more time underwater, both freediving and scuba diving, I thought the hardest part would be learning all the skills. Holding my breath, clearing my mask, using the equipment, controlling my buoyancy. But I realized pretty quickly that the biggest thing I had to learn was how to stay calm.

Underwater, everything changes. The sounds are different, you move differently, and you have to pay attention to things you normally never think about. With scuba, you learn to trust your equipment and your training. With freediving, you learn to trust your body and know your limits.

The more time I spent underwater, the more comfortable it became. Instead of fighting every nervous feeling, I learned to slow down, think clearly, and enjoy what was around me.

Now being underwater is one of my favorite places to be. Whether I have a tank on my back or I’m diving down on one breath, it’s taught me something I didn’t expect: being confident doesn’t always mean being fearless. Sometimes it just means staying calm enough to keep learning.
