What Growing Up on a Sailboat Actually Feels Like

Most people think of school as classrooms, desks, textbooks, and teachers. My school doesn’t always look like that. Sometimes it’s an island I’ve never been to before, a reef underneath the boat, a conversation with someone who grew up completely differently than I did, or a place I had only seen on a map until we actually sailed there.

Living and traveling on a sailboat has shown me that learning doesn’t stop when you close a textbook. When we arrive somewhere new, I want to know why the island looks the way it does, who lives there, what happened there before we arrived, and what life is like for the people who call it home. History feels different when you can actually stand where it happened, and geography makes a lot more sense when you had to cross the water to get there.

Some of my favorite lessons happen underwater. I can read about coral reefs, fish, currents, and marine life, but seeing them for myself makes me want to understand them more. The ocean becomes this giant classroom where there is always something new to notice. Sometimes the questions I come back with are more interesting than the ones I started with.

Worldschooling has also taught me things that are harder to find in a textbook. I’ve had to meet new people, try things I wasn’t comfortable with, figure things out when plans changed, and learn how different people live around the world. Every new place reminds me that the way I’m used to doing something isn’t the only way to do it.

I still have normal schoolwork, assignments, and things I have to study just like everyone else. The difference is that sometimes what I’m learning suddenly becomes real right in front of me. An island stops being a dot on a map. The ocean stops being a chapter in a science book. A different culture stops being something I’m just reading about.

That’s probably my favorite part of growing up this way. I don’t just get to learn about the world. I get to go out and experience it.
