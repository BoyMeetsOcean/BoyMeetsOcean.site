What Growing Up on a Sailboat Actually Feels Like

People sometimes think growing up on a sailboat means I’m basically on vacation all the time. And I understand why. I wake up surrounded by water, swim off the back of my house, explore islands, dive, fish, sail to new places, and get to see parts of the world that a lot of people only see in pictures. There are definitely days when it feels pretty unreal. But living on a sailboat is also a lot more normal—and a lot more work—than it probably looks from the outside.

Our boat isn’t just something we take out for the weekend. It’s our home. That means when something breaks, we can’t just leave it at the marina and go back to our house. If the weather gets bad, we’re living in it. If something needs to be cleaned, fixed, tied down, or checked, everyone has to help. Sometimes plans completely change because of the wind, weather, tides, or something on the boat that needs attention. You learn pretty quickly that the ocean doesn’t really care what your plans were.

There are also things about normal life that are completely different. My backyard can be Massachusetts one month and somewhere hundreds of miles away the next. Instead of driving somewhere, sometimes we sail there. I’ve had dolphins swimming next to the boat, jumped into water so clear I could see the bottom, explored places I had never heard of before we arrived, and watched sunsets from the deck that I probably wouldn’t have stopped to notice if this wasn’t my life.

But I think the biggest thing growing up this way has taught me is how to adapt. Things don’t always go according to plan, and you can’t control everything around you. You learn to solve problems, help out, stay calm when things get difficult, and be comfortable with not always knowing exactly what comes next.

Sometimes I miss things that would be easier if we lived in a regular house. But then I think about everything I would have missed if we had never done this. Growing up on a sailboat isn’t a permanent vacation, and it definitely isn’t always easy. It’s just my version of growing up—and even with the storms, the work, the broken stuff, and all the unexpected changes, I wouldn’t trade it.
